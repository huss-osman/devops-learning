# LAN And WAN

## Overview

This section introduces the different types of **computer networks**, focusing on **Local Area Networks (LAN)** and **Wide Area Networks (WAN)**. It explains how networks are structured based on size, scope, and how devices are connected.

This is useful in real-world environments where understanding the difference between **local networks** and **global networks** is essential. These concepts form the foundation for how systems communicate across **infrastructure** and the **internet**.

---

## Concepts Covered

### Local Area Network (LAN)

A **Local Area Network (LAN)** connects devices within a limited geographical area such as a home, office, school, or data centre. LANs are typically privately managed and provide fast communication between connected devices.

---

### Wide Area Network (WAN)

A **Wide Area Network (WAN)** connects multiple LANs across larger geographical areas such as cities, countries, or continents. WANs rely on telecommunications infrastructure and internet service providers to enable communication between networks.

---

### Network Scope

The scope of a network refers to the geographical area it covers. LANs operate within small local environments, whereas WANs are designed to support communication across much larger distances.

---

### Connectivity Between Networks

Devices on the same LAN can communicate directly with each other, while communication between different LANs usually requires routers and WAN connections to transfer traffic between networks.

---

### Performance Differences

LANs generally provide lower latency and higher speeds because devices are physically closer together. WANs often experience higher latency due to the greater distances that data must travel.

---

### Network Ownership and Management

LANs are usually owned and managed by a single organisation or individual, whereas WAN infrastructure is commonly managed by internet service providers and telecommunications companies.

---

### The Internet as a WAN

The **internet** is the largest example of a WAN, connecting millions of smaller networks across the world and allowing global communication between systems and services.

---

### Cloud and Enterprise Networking

Modern cloud environments and enterprise infrastructures rely on both LAN and WAN technologies. Data centres use LANs for internal communication, while WANs connect users, offices, and cloud services across different locations.

---

## Commands

This section is concept-focused, but these tools will later be used to apply your knowledge:

- `ip a` → View **network interfaces and IP addresses**  
- `ping` → Test **connectivity within a network**  
- `traceroute` → Trace how traffic moves across **networks**  
- `netstat` / `ss` → Inspect **network connections**  

---

## Key Takeaways

- **LAN (Local Area Network)** connects devices within a small area (home, office)  
- **WAN (Wide Area Network)** connects multiple LANs across larger regions  
- LANs are typically faster and locally managed, while WANs span wider distances  
- The **internet** is the largest example of a WAN  
- Understanding network types is key for designing and troubleshooting systems
